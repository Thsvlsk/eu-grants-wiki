How to record working time and convert hours into day-equivalents

All programmes using the EU model grant agreementLast reviewed 2026-10-15

In short

In the AGA, time records are the proof of the time an employee worked on the action. By default this is a declaration of day-equivalents signed every month; another reliable time-recording system, on paper or on a computer, is also accepted AGA v2.0, pp. 205 and 209.

The short answer

Time worked on the action must be supported by declarations signed monthly by the employee and their supervisor, unless another reliable time-record system is in place p. 205. If you record hours, divide the hours worked on the action in the reporting period by the hours of one day-equivalent, and round to the nearest half-day p. 210. Example: 800 hours with an 8-hour day-equivalent give 100 day-equivalents p. 211.

How it works

  1. Choose how to record. For every person who works for the action, full-time or part-time, exclusively or not, you may either sign a monthly declaration on day-equivalents worked for the action (the default, with a template), or use another paper- or computer-based reliable time recording system that records at least all the time worked for the action p. 209.
  2. Sign at least monthly. Reliable time records must be dated and signed at least monthly by the person working for the action and their supervisor. In a computer-based system the signatures may be electronic, with a documented and secure process for managing user rights and an auditable log p. 209.
  3. Do not round in the monthly declaration. Record the exact share of the day: a person with a standard working day of 8 hours who worked 2 hours on the action in a month records 0.25 day-equivalents for that month p. 209.
  4. If you record hours, choose the hours of a day-equivalent with one of the three options below, and apply it consistently pp. 210 and 211.
  5. Convert once per person per reporting period: hours worked on the action ÷ hours of a day-equivalent, rounded to the nearest half-day, e.g. 17.79 = 18 and 17.64 = 17.5 pp. 210 and 211.

The AGA's recommendation. It recommends exploring monthly declarations of days, because they limit the record-keeping burden and avoid converting hours into day-equivalents p. 209.

Option 3 is for organisations whose usual cost accounting practice determines the standard number of annual productive hours of a full-time employee p. 211.

When the working hours fluctuate over the year, for example 8 hours per day in all months except 7 hours per day in July and August, the hours of a day-equivalent are: [(working hours per day × number of months to which it applies) + (working hours per day × number of months to which it applies)] ÷ 12, then divided by the working time factor pp. 211 and 212. The AGA does not give the result for this illustration.

Watch out

Round the cost calculation, not the monthly declaration.

Parallel systems must be consistent. If you keep parallel time record systems, e.g. weekly time-sheets of hours and monthly declarations of days, they must be consistent.

In case of significant discrepancies discovered e.g. during checks, reviews and audits, only the set of documents recording the lower amount of days will be accepted.

— AGA v2.0, Art. 20, p. 211

Working days are the standard ones, not the days actually worked. In option 2b, the number of working days per week (or month) is the standard number as per your usual practices and applicable rules, usually 5 per week, and the same for full-time and part-time employees p. 210. The AGA's example: a 50% part-time employee who works two and a half days a week still uses 5, not 2.5 or 3 p. 210.

Depends on your programme

Who this article covers. Article 20 applies to all beneficiaries. The AGA also says the granting authority may accept alternative evidence of the time worked for the action, if it considers that it offers an adequate level of assurance p. 205.
