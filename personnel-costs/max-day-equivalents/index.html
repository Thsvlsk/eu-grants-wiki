How to work out the maximum declarable day-equivalents

Employees (A.1): all programmes except SMP ESS and CUST/FISCLast reviewed 2026-10-15

In short

The maximum declarable day-equivalents are the cap on the day-equivalents you use to calculate a person's daily rate in a reporting period. You determine them first, then work out the daily rate AGA v2.0, p. 53.

The short answer

For each person and reporting period, the maximum is ((215 / 12) × number of months employed within the period) × working time factor, rounded to the nearest half day-equivalent AGA v2.0, p. 53. A month counts as 30 days. If the working time factor changes during the period, you calculate the stretches separately and add them up.

How it works

  1. Count the months. Use the length of the reporting period, or the length of the person's employment within it if that is shorter, for example for someone newly hired or no longer employed p. 53.
  2. A month has 30 days. This holds for all personnel cost calculations, so a part month is the number of days divided by 30 p. 53.
  3. Set the working time factor: 1 for full-time, 0.5 for 50% part-time, and so on p. 53.
  4. Apply the formula and round to the nearest half day-equivalent p. 53.
  5. If the working time factor changes during the reporting period, you calculate the maximum separately for the months before and after the change, and add them up p. 53.
  6. Alternatively, calculate by calendar year. A footnote on page 52 allows it, if consistently applied: read the footnote.

Watch out

This is not the 215-day ceiling. The maximum above is per reporting period. On top of it there is a horizontal ceiling that counts all your EU grants together:

the total number of day-equivalents declared across EU grants for the person can NOT be higher than 215 per calendar year

— AGA v2.0, Art. 6.2.A.1, p. 53

The AGA's example p. 53: in a reporting period from 01/07/2022 to 31/12/2023, a 50% part-time employee hired on 01/08/2022 has a horizontal ceiling of 45 day-equivalents for 2022 (rounded) and 107.5 for 2023.

Round the cost calculation, not the monthly declaration. The rounding to the nearest half day-equivalent applies to the day-equivalents you declare and to the maximum. The monthly declaration is different:

it is NOT allowed to round up or down to the nearest half day-equivalent in the monthly declaration.

— AGA v2.0, Art. 20, p. 209

Depends on your programme

Check your Data Sheet

Parental leave is one of the cost eligibility options listed in Point 3 of the Data Sheet p. 22. It only applies to you if it is in your Grant Agreement.

Horizon Europe · Humanitarian Aid only

Parental leave. If this option is activated in the Grant Agreement, days on parental leave during the reporting period may be deducted: you reduce the maximum declarable day-equivalents by the number of day-equivalents spent on parental leave p. 55.

Other leaves or absences can NOT be deducted, including long-term sick leave, breastfeeding leave and leave to take care of a sick child.

— AGA v2.0, Art. 6.2.A.1, p. 55

The AGA's example p. 55: a reporting period of 14 months, with 4 months of parental leave.
