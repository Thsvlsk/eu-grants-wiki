How to calculate the daily rate for personnel costs

Employees (A.1): all programmes except SMP ESS and CUST/FISCLast reviewed 2026-10-15

In short

In the AGA, the daily rate is the cost of one day of an employee's work. It must be calculated only from what the employee actually cost you, limited to salary, social security contributions, taxes and other costs linked to the remuneration. Multiplied by the days worked on the action, it gives the amount you declare AGA v2.0, pp. 49 and 52.

The short answer

Divide what the employee actually cost you in the months of the reporting period by the maximum declarable day-equivalents for those months p. 54. Example: an employee who cost EUR 75,000 over an 18-month reporting period, with a maximum of 269 declarable day-equivalents, has a daily rate of EUR 75,000 ÷ 269 = EUR 278.81 p. 54. You do this once per reporting period for each employee p. 52.

How it works

  1. Pick the person and the period. You must normally do one calculation per reporting period, for each person who worked on the action p. 52.
  2. Add up the actual personnel costs of the months within the period, as recorded in your (statutory) accounts until the end of that period p. 54.
  3. Get the maximum declarable day-equivalents for the same months: how to work them out.
  4. Divide the costs by that maximum. You have to use this formula irrespective of the situation: full-time, part-time, partial hire, etc. p. 54. The result is the daily rate.
  5. Multiply by the days worked. These are the day-equivalents actually worked on the action, rounded to the nearest half-day and recorded in the monthly declarations or in your time-recording system p. 52.

Worked example

The AGA's worked example, step by step

Reporting period from 01/12/2021 to 31/05/2023 (18 months). The person works 50% part-time for 6 months and full-time for the next 12 AGA v2.0, p. 54.

StepWhat you doCalculationResult
1. Person and periodSame person, one reporting period6 months part-time + 12 months full-time18 months
2. Actual personnel costsAdd up the costs recorded in the accountsEUR 15,000 (6 months part-time) + EUR 60,000 (12 months full-time)EUR 75,000
3. Maximum declarable day-equivalentsCalculate separately before and after the change, then add uppart-time: ((215 / 12) × 6) × 0.5 = 53.75; full-time: ((215 / 12) × 12) × 1 = 215; 53.75 + 215269 (rounded)
4. Daily rateDivide the costs by the maximum75,000 ÷ 269EUR 278.81
5. Multiply by the days workedMultiply the daily rate by the day-equivalents workedThe AGA does not give the days worked in this example

Why there is a maximum. In the AGA's words: "Since you may not declare more than 100% of your personnel cost, the number of declarable day-equivalents in each reporting period is capped" p. 53.

Whole period or each calendar year

A footnote on page 52 allows the second option:

Alternatively, the calculation may be done separately for each calendar year within the reporting period, if this is consistently applied. In that case, the 'number of months within the reporting period' referred to in the formulas is to be understood as the number of months of the respective calendar year that are within the reporting period.

— AGA v2.0, Art. 6.2.A.1, p. 52

The AGA does not say what "consistently applied" means in this footnote.

Watch out

The contract text and the annotations word it differently.

The daily rate must be calculated as: {annual personnel costs for the person divided by 215}

— AGA v2.0, Art. 6.2.A.1 (contract text), p. 49

Section 1.4 of the annotations pp. 52 to 54 sets out the calculation per reporting period, as shown above.

Two different caps. The maximum above is per reporting period. A separate horizontal ceiling counts all your EU grants together p. 53: see how the two fit together.

Costs after the end of the action, for example to prepare the final report. You use the daily rate of the last reporting period and add the extra days to that period. The maximum declarable day-equivalents does not apply to those additional days pp. 54 and 55.

Depends on your programme

Who this article covers. Employees under Article 6.2.A.1, in:

all programmes except SMP ESS, CUST/FISC or where declared ineligible in the call conditions

— AGA v2.0, Art. 6.2.A.1, p. 50

Check your Data Sheet

Some cases change the calculation, depending on options in your Grant Agreement. The Data Sheet lists them in Point 3, under "Cost eligibility options" p. 22: parental leave, supplementary payments (standard or project-based) and average personnel costs.

Check the Data Sheet of your Grant Agreement, if specific options apply.

— AGA v2.0, Art. 6.2.A.1, p. 52

Horizon Europe · Humanitarian Aid only

Parental leave. If this option is activated in your Grant Agreement, days on parental leave during the reporting period may be deducted when calculating the maximum declarable day-equivalents and the daily rate p. 55. See how.

Horizon Europe only

Project-based remuneration. If this option is activated in the Grant Agreement, beneficiaries may use a specific calculation method for the daily rate p. 56.

Horizon Europe · Digital Europe · Connecting Europe Facility · European Defence Fund · Union Civil Protection Mechanism · Humanitarian Aid only

Average personnel costs. If this option is activated in the Grant Agreement, beneficiaries who consistently calculate average rates for their staff as part of their analytical cost accounting system can use these average rates for the daily rate p. 59.
