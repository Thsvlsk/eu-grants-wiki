What is the 215-day ceiling?

Employees (A.1), with specific rules for other personnel typesLast reviewed 2026-10-15

In short

In the AGA, the 215-day ceiling (the "horizontal ceiling") is the limit on the day-equivalents declared for one person across all EU grants together in a calendar year: 215, or 215 multiplied by the working time factor for part-time staff AGA v2.0, pp. 49 and 53.

The short answer

Across all your EU grants, the day-equivalents declared for one person can NOT be higher than 215 per calendar year, or the pro-rata with the working time factor p. 53. Example: a 50% part-time employee hired on 01/08/2022 has a ceiling of 45 day-equivalents for 2022 and 107.5 for 2023 p. 53. It is separate from the maximum declarable day-equivalents, which caps each reporting period p. 53.

How it works

  1. Take one person and one calendar year. The ceiling counts per calendar year, not per reporting period p. 53.
  2. Add up the day-equivalents declared for that person in all EU grants in that year p. 53.
  3. Compare with 215, or the pro-rata by multiplying 215 with the working time factor p. 53. For part of a year, the AGA's example uses the months employed: (215 / 12 × months) × working time factor p. 53.
  4. The total can NOT be higher. The ceiling exists to avoid double-funding of personnel cost p. 53.

Worked example

Reporting period from 01/07/2022 to 31/12/2023

A 50% part-time employee hired on 01/08/2022 AGA v2.0, p. 53. The reporting period covers two calendar years, so there is one ceiling per year.

Calendar yearCalculationCeiling
2022 (5 months employed in the reporting period)(215 / 12 × 5) × 0.545 (rounded)

Watch out

The contract text says it in one line.

The total number of day-equivalents declared in EU grants, for a person for a year, cannot be higher than 215

— AGA v2.0, Art. 6.2.A.1 (contract text), p. 49

The annotations specify "per calendar year" and the pro-rata with the working time factor p. 53.

When a reporting period crosses two calendar years, each calendar year has its own ceiling, as in the AGA's example above p. 53.

Depends on your programme

Horizon Europe · Humanitarian Aid only

Parental leave. If this option is in your Grant Agreement, the ceiling is 215 minus time spent on parental leave (if any) p. 49.

Horizon Europe only

HE personnel unit costs. The horizontal ceiling applies not only in case of work on multiple actions, but also where the person worked in only one EU grant during a given calendar year p. 73.

SME owners and natural person beneficiaries (unit costs). The same applies: the ceiling counts even where the person worked in only one EU grant during a given calendar year p. 66.

Natural persons with a direct contract and seconded persons (A.2, A.3). You must respect the horizontal ceiling if a pro-rata number of 215 annual day-equivalents is used in the calculation of the daily rate p. 63. The AGA lists this as a clarification in version 2.0 p. 4.

Horizon Europe · Digital Europe · European Defence Fund only

Internally invoiced goods and services. The share of the working time of a person that is included in the calculation of the unit cost reduces the number of day-equivalents that the person can charge over the year to EU grants p. 112.
