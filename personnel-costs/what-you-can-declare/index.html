What personnel costs can you declare?

Employees (A.1): all programmes except SMP ESS and CUST/FISCLast reviewed 2026-10-15

In short

In the AGA, personnel costs for employees are the costs of people who work for your organisation under an employment contract, or an equivalent appointing act for civil servants, and who are assigned to the action AGA v2.0, p. 50.

The short answer

Employee costs must be limited to salaries, social security contributions, taxes and other costs linked to the remuneration, if they arise from national law or the employment contract p. 49. Only employees assigned to the action count, and bonuses only if paid on objective conditions and in a consistent manner pp. 50 and 51. Agency staff, seconded staff and self-employed people are not declared here p. 50.

How it works

  1. Check the contract. The person works for you under an employment contract or an equivalent appointing act. For civil servants, who do not sign employment contracts, the official nomination for their post counts p. 50.
  2. Check the assignment. Only personnel assigned to the action count, that is, working for the project according to internal written instructions, an organisation chart or another documented management decision p. 50.
  3. Check the general conditions. The costs must fulfil the general eligibility conditions (incurred during the action duration, necessary, etc.; Article 6.1) and be paid in accordance with national law, the collective labour agreement and the employment contract p. 51.
  4. Add up the eligible items in the lists below p. 51.
  5. Declare them as actual personnel costs, the standard case, through the daily rate: how to calculate it. Average personnel costs are the alternative, only if that option is in your Grant Agreement pp. 50 and 51.

What goes in, what stays out

Generally, you may include p. 51:

  • fixed salary
  • fixed complements, if they are unconditional entitlements for the person (e.g. family allowance and contributions to medical insurance schemes set out in national law, complementary pension plan contributions set out in the collective labour agreement)
  • variable complements, e.g. bonuses, if they are paid based on objective conditions set out, at least, in your internal rules, and paid in a consistent manner, e.g. not just for actions supported by EU grants
  • social security contributions (mandatory employer and employee contributions)
  • taxes linked to the remuneration (e.g. income tax withholding)
  • other costs and payments linked to the remuneration, if justified and registered as personnel costs in line with your usual remuneration practices (e.g. company car made available for private use, lunch vouchers, accrual for unconditional severance payments mandatory under national law)

Components that are legally obligatory under national law, the employment contract or a similar act can also be included, for example the thirteenth salary or Christmas pay p. 54.

You may NOT include p. 51:

  • any part of the remuneration that has not been an actual cost for you
  • payments of dividends to employees
  • variable complements based on commercial targets or fund raising targets
  • arbitrary bonuses (not paid based on objective conditions set out, at least, in your internal rules, or not paid in a consistent manner)
  • bonuses that depend on budget availability on the specific project (e.g. paid only if there are remaining funds in the budget of a project)

Watch out

Costs someone else paid for are not your costs.

any part of the remuneration which has not been an actual cost for you (for example, salaries reimbursed by a social security scheme or a private insurance in case of long sick leave or maternity leave)

— AGA v2.0, Art. 6.2.A.1, p. 51

Agency staff are not personnel costs.

Such staff can NOT be charged under this budget category because contracts with temporary work agencies qualify typically as purchase of services

— AGA v2.0, Art. 6.2.A.1, p. 56

The costs can be charged under B. Subcontracting or C.3 Other goods, works and services, if they comply with the eligibility conditions of those categories p. 56.

Proof of the assignment. The monthly declaration of days worked, correctly signed, or reliable time records will normally be sufficient proof that the person was assigned to the action, unless there is contradicting evidence, for example an employment contract saying the person was hired to work on another project p. 50.

Teleworking days are accepted if you have clear rules allowing teleworking, and the teleworked days were in line with those rules, for example not above the maximum days of teleworking your rules allow p. 55.

The costs must match your records. They must be detailed for each person working for the action and match your accounting records and supporting documents, such as labour contracts, payslips, time records and bank statements showing salary payments (AGA v2.0, Art. 20, p. 207).

Depends on your programme

Who this article covers. Employees under Article 6.2.A.1, in:

all programmes except SMP ESS, CUST/FISC or where declared ineligible in the call conditions

— AGA v2.0, Art. 6.2.A.1, p. 50

Check your Data Sheet

Some options change what goes into personnel costs, depending on your Grant Agreement. The Data Sheet lists them in Point 3, under "Cost eligibility options" p. 22: parental leave, supplementary payments (standard or project-based) and average personnel costs.

Horizon Europe · Humanitarian Aid only

Parental leave. If this option is in your Grant Agreement, the salaries include net payments during parental leave p. 49.

Horizon Europe · Digital Europe · European Defence Fund · Connecting Europe Facility · Union Civil Protection Mechanism · Humanitarian Aid only

Average personnel costs. If this option is in your Grant Agreement, employee costs are declared as unit costs in accordance with your usual cost accounting practices ('average personnel costs'), instead of actual personnel costs pp. 50 and 51.
